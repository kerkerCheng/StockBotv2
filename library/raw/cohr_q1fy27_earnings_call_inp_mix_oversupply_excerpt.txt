"This is Jeff Opsenon for Ryan. Thank you for the question. [1:02:31 James Anderson, Chief Executive Officer]
"Yeah, thanks, Jeff. We haven't broken out the split, but I can tell you the split hasn't changed much in a year. We're using about the same mix of internal versus external now as we were a year ago. So although we've doubled our internal indium phosphide supply, the primary usage of our internal indium phosphide parts is in our data center transceivers. If you look at the growth rate of our data center business, data center transceivers, it's basically tracked the growth of our indium phosphide capacity, although basically one quarter later, because in the quarter we produce the lasers, the next quarter is when we ship the transceivers. And so essentially our transceivers have been businesses grown at the same rate as our internal indium phosphide, which means that our mix of internal versus external really hasn't changed much over the past year. Now, as we continue to expand indium phosphide now looking forward, more than doubling it by the end of 27 , we do believe over the coming quarters we'll start to shift more of the mix from external towards internal. Now, we always believe we'll have some portion of our supply from external for our transceiver business, but we believe over the coming quarters we'll start to shift more towards internal. And that's really because it's a tremendous cost advantage for us to use our internally produced lasers, especially the ones that are made on the six-inch platform. And in my prepared remarks when I said, you know, the majority of the benefit from six-inch Indian phosphide is ahead of us, that's actually what really what I'm referring to is that benefit of changing the mix towards internal is largely ahead of us, and that's a much bigger cost structure improvement."
