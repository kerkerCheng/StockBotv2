- Demand stayed strong: Management said the order strength was not driven by prebuying or tariffs, but by real demand and longer lead times in markets like heat exchangers, CO2 refrigeration, aerospace and defense, and data center cooling.
- Margins improving: Q1 margin performance was helped by volume leverage, pricing actions, productivity, and mix, while some businesses are still being held back by temporary capacity and facility-consolidation costs.
- Big growth areas: Clean Energy & Fueling and Climate & Sustainability Technologies stood out, with organic growth of 11% and 15% respectively, while Dover pointed to AI, power infrastructure, liquid cooling, and CO2 refrigeration as major growth engines.
- Lead times and capacity Management said stronger demand is pushing lead times longer in several businesses, especially aerospace and defense, steam and gas turbine components, CO2 refrigeration systems, and heat exchangers.
- In several cases, customers are booking earlier simply to reserve production capacity, and Dover said it is adding capacity where demand and returns are strongest.
- Heat exchangers benefited from demand tied to liquid cooling and data centers, while CO2 refrigeration and refrigerated door cases also improved.
- Management said defense demand remains strong, but capacity is still the main constraint, and it is working to add production without sacrificing discipline.
- During the quarter, we continued to return capital to shareholders through opportunistic share repurchases while also investing behind high-return capacity expansions and productivity projects.
- Heat exchangers performed especially well across all regions, particularly in North America on the growth in liquid cooling applications and data centers.
- Margins were up in the quarter on volume leverage and a higher mix of CO2 systems and heat exchangers.
