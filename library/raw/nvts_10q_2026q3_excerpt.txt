[Note — Execution of At-The-Market Sales Agreements]
During the three months ended June 30, 2026, the Company sold approximately 17.4 million shares of Class A common stock in at-the-market offerings, for gross proceeds of approximately $380.7 million and offering-related costs of approximately $7.6 million. The sales were made under two Sales Agreements: the first, entered into on May 11, 2026, terminated on May 12, 2026 upon completion of its $ 125.0 million offering, and the second, entered into on June 8, 2026, provides for sales of up to $ 500.0 million. No shares were sold under either agreement during the three months ended September 30, 2026, and approximately $244.3 million remained available under the second agreement as of September 30, 2026.

[Note — Acquisition of Claros, Inc.]
On October 6, 2026 , the Company acquired all of the outstanding equity interests of Claros, Inc. (“Claros”), a developer of power management solutions for artificial intelligence data-center applications , pursuant to an Agreement and Plan of Merger dated August 24, 2026.
Consideration transferred consisted of approximately $ 126 million in cash, net of approximately $ 9.4 million of cash acquired, subject to customary post-closing adjustments, and 6,912,669 shares of Class A common stock with an acquisition-date fair value of approximately $ 82.7 million

[Note — Legal proceedings]
On or about July 7, 2026, Wolfspeed filed a patent infringement lawsuit in the United States District Court for the District of Delaware against the Company and several of its affiliates. The lawsuit alleges that certain Navitas GaN and SiC products infringe multiple Wolfspeed patents
On or about July 22, 2026, Renesas Electronics Corporation and Renesas Electronics America Inc. (together, “Renesas”) filed a lawsuit against the Company and two of the Company’s employees who are former employees of Renesas, including the Company’s chief executive officer, alleging misappropriation of trade secrets, breach of contract and other claims.

[MD&A — Overview]
We focus primarily on high-power markets, including AI data centers, energy and grid infrastructure, performance computing, and industrial electrification.
Our revenue represents the sale of semiconductors through specialized distributors to original equipment manufacturers, their suppliers, and other end customers. We consider the domicile of our end customers, rather than the distributors we sell to directly, to be the basis of attributing revenues from external customers to individual countries.
