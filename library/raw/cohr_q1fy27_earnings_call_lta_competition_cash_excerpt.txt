[1:09:58 James Anderson, Chief Executive Officer]
"Yeah, thanks, Carl. The answer to the last part of the question is yes, definitely. Maybe I'll start with the end of the question. So we've signed a number of LTAs over the past quarters. The one public LTA is the one with NVIDIA that we signed back in March, but we've signed a number of other LTAs. The LTAs all follow a general pattern, and one part of the LTA is typically some sort of upfront payment from the customer."
"It could be either prepayment or NRE. There's different forms of payment, but there's some sort of upfront payment from the customer. And what we do that for is for two reasons. Number one is it makes sure that the customers have skin in the game. But number two is we use that upfront payment to help fund the CapEx to build the capacity for that customer, right? We think it's important that if we're going to go build the capacity that the customers either fund that or help co-fund that. And so far we've had customers be very, very willing to do that. And I think that's worked really well for us. And so I would say almost every LTA that we've signed has that general pattern."
"And so that certainly gives us better confidence and helps offset the risk in terms of our own capital deployment. But I guess on the first part of your question, we certainly work very, very closely with our customers on planning out the demand. I think one thing to keep in mind with Indian phosphide capacity is remember that it's not just being driven by data center demand. There's two things driving optics, in general, optics demand, right? Number one is data center growth, but number two is, in the case of integrated optics, it's the conversion of the copper links to optical over time. And that is a technology trend that, in my view, is independent of the market demand."

[45:49 Simon Leopold, Analyst, Raymond James — 問 Marvell 併 Lumentum]
"And as a follow-up, I have a hard question, one that I imagine you anticipate getting, is what does the proposed consolidation of Lumentum and Marvell mean to you? Thank you."

[46:02 James Anderson, Chief Executive Officer]
"Yeah, thanks, Simon. No, I don't think that's a hard question at all. I think happy to answer that. I'd give a few thoughts on this. I think number one is it definitely demonstrates that optics and photonics is becoming an absolutely critical part of data center architecture. You know, as we look forward, as we convert the entire scale-up network and then even the scale-in chip-to-chip communication links, over time, all of that getting converted from copper to optical, photonic technology is absolutely critical to making that happen, to driving the innovation in data center architecture. So I think it's a proof point around just the criticality of optics and photonics. And then I think for Coherent, I think it just highlights the tremendous strategic value of the portfolio that is within Coherent."
"There's no other company, I believe, on the planet that has a broader and deeper set of photonic and optical technology. if you look at the entire optical signal chain from the generation of the light to the transmission through our fiber optic cable that we make to detection on the other side, I don't think there's any other company that's got a better portfolio of technology. So I think it definitely highlights the strategic importance of our technology platform. And then, look, I think from a customer perspective, I think that for a lot of our customers, I think it's really important that they have an independent photonic optical supplier and partner. And so I think it positions us really well to be that independent optical supplier that's going to support, without any competitive conflict, can support multiple different customers and support both hyperscaler customers, can support semiconductor customers."
