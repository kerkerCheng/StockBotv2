I. Letter to Shareholders 
Dear Shareholders, 
In 2025, driven by the rapid growth in global demand for artificial intelligence (AI), shipments 
of data center–related products increased significantly, contributing to the Company’s revenue 
growth. At the same time, the Company has actively optimized its supply chain management 
processes, implemented AI -driven production systems, and enhanced production planning. 
Through accelerating product development timelines and improving production efficiency, the 
Company aims to meet customers ’ rapidly growing demand and capture emerging market 
opportunities. 
A. 2025 Annual Operating Report 
1. Results of Business Plan Implementation 
The Company ’s operating revenue for the full year 2025 reached NT$2,202,822 
thousand, representin g an increase of 82.3% compared to 2024. This growth was 
primarily driven by the continued expansion of demand for artificial intelligence (AI) 
and data center–related applications, which significantly boosted shipments of silicon 
photonics products. 
Net income after tax for 2025 amounted to NT$428,401 thousand, with a net profit 
margin of 19.4% and earnings per share (EPS) of NT$4.66. Profitability improved 
substantially compared to 2024.

4. Research and Development Overview 
The Company’s investment in research and development amounted to NT$271,715 
thousand. R&D efforts were focused on high -speed silicon photonics products for 
data center applications at 800G and  1.6T and above, as well as high -speed EML 
components at 50G/100G and above. The Company also continues to collaborate 
with customers on the development of next -generation applications, including 3D 
sensing, LiDAR, and FTTH products, in order to strengthen  its long -term 
technological competitiveness and product portfolio. 
B. Summary of the Business Plan for Fiscal Year 2026 
1. Business Policies 
Looking ahead to 2026, the continued expansion of artificial intelligence (AI) 
applications is expected to drive steady growth in demand for high -speed, low -
latency data transmission in cloud computing and high -performance computing 
(HPC). Data center transmission standards are evolving from 400G to 800G and 1.6T 
and above, increasing demand for high -speed optical transceiv ers and key 
optoelectronic components. The momentum for data center construction in major 
markets such as the United States and China is expected to continue. In addition, as 
silicon photonics technology accelerates toward commercialization, mid - to long-
term growth opportunities for advanced laser component suppliers are becoming 
increasingly evident. 
The Company will continue to focus on core technologies in laser epitaxy and 
components for high-end optical communications and data center applications, while 
strengthening mass production capabilities and process integration. The following 
business strategies will be implemented to support steady growth in operational scale 
and overall performance: 
 Leveraging Mass Production Advantages to Expand Customer Base and Shipment 
V olume 
The Company has established a flexible and highly efficient mass production 
model capable of rapidly responding to diverse product specifications and order 
requirements, while consistently delivering competitive quality and lead times . 
Going forward, the Company will build on its mass production experience, 
deepen relationships with existing customers, and expand its customer base. By 
supporting customers in adopting diverse end applications, the Company aims to 
increase shipment volume and market share as demand grows. 
3 
 
 Maintaining Technological Leadership to Enhance Core Competitiveness 
The Company will continue investing in the development of high -speed laser 
components for optical communications and data center applications. It will also 
actively expand into FTTH, automotive LiDAR, long -wavelength 3D sensing, 
and industrial sensing applications, collaborating with customers to develop next-
generation high -performance laser and key components. In addition, by 
establishing an AI-enabled smart manufacturing platform that integrates process 
data analysis and automated management, the Company will accelerate the 
introduction of advanced and large-size products, flexibly allocate capacity based 
on market demand, and enhance project execution  capabilities and overall 
competitiveness. 
 Deepening Process Integration to Strengthen Production Resilience 
The Company will continue to optimize and integrate manufacturing processes to 
improve capacity utilization, while enhancing quality control, yield  rates, and 
production scheduling. In addition to strengthening key technologies such as 
front-end processes and multi-stage epitaxial growth to meet the demand for high-
quality laser components in high -speed optical communications, the Company 
will also c ollaborate strategically with multiple outsourcing partners to provide 
end-to-end manufacturing services —from epitaxy to back -end processing —
thereby improving supply flexibility and delivery stability to meet diverse 
customer requirements. 
2. Estimated Sales V olume and Basis 
The Company’s estimated sales volume for 2026 has been determined based on a 
comprehensive assessment of major customers ’ demand forecasts, industry 
development trends, market supply and demand conditions, as well as the Company’s 
capacity planning and business strategies. The Company will continue to make 
rolling adjustments in response to actual market conditions and customer demand to 
maintain operational flexibility and effective risk management. 
3. Key Production and Sales Policies 
a. Continuously improve product quality and reliability 
b. Strengthen core technologies and product development to increase the proportion 
of high value-added products in revenue 
c. Enhance production efficiency and capacity utilization through process 
optimization and s mart manufacturing, reduce unit costs, and improve delivery 
capabilities. 
  
4 
 
C. Future Development Strategy 
In response to market trends in high -speed optical communications, AI, and emerging 
sensing applications, the Company will adhere to a prudent management approach, 
strengthen its core technologies and supply chain resilience, and implement the following 
mid- to long-term growth strategies: 
1. Ensuring Stable Supply of Raw Materials and Substrates 
Through diversified sourcing, strategic procurement, and inventory management, the 
Company aims to mitigate the risk of supply disruptions and ensure a stable supply 
of key materials required for mass production and advanced product development. 
2. Strengthening AI and Data Center Supply Chain Positioning 
The C ompany will continue to focus on high -speed optical communications and 
silicon photonics-related laser epitaxy and component technologies, actively expand 
into AI server and data center supply chains, and enhance collaboration with 
international customers to increase visibility and market share in high -end 
applications. 
3. Leveraging AI Tools to Achieve Smart Manufacturing and Advance Product 
Development 
While maintaining stable mass production and shipment, the Company will continue 
to invest in advanced manu facturing processes and high value -added product 
development. Through product mix optimization and flexible capacity allocation, the 
Company aims to improve operational efficiency and sustain long -term growth, 
while keeping pace with evolving industry standards. 
4. Enhancing Talent Development and Organizational Capabilities 
The Company will continue to promote employee training, professional skill 
development, and cross -functional collaboration to cultivate globally competitive 
R&D and manufacturing teams, thereby improving overall organizational efficiency. 
D. Impact of External Competition, Regulatory Environment, and Overall Business 
Environment 
Global political and economic conditions remain highly uncertain. The U.S. –China 
technology rivalry, adjustments in trade policies, and geopolitical risks continue to affect 
the technology industry’s supply chains and market demand. At the same time, inflation, 
elevated interest rates, and slowing economic growth have increased operational 
challenges. 
In response to cha nges in the external environment, the Company will enhance 
operational flexibility and strengthen risk identification and management. Through 
5 
 
market diversification, supply chain resilience management, and prudent capital 
allocation, the Company aims to navigate uncertainties with stability.  
From a regulatory perspective, the Company will align with evolving policies and 
regulations, improve internal governance and operational processes to ensure compliance, 
and continue to promote ESG initiatives. These efforts are intended to strengthen 
corporate governance and information transparency, thereby enhancing the Company ’s 
long-term sustainability.  
Lastly, we extend our most sincere gratitude to our shareholders for their long -term 
support, and we hope you will continue to provide us with your va luable guidance and 
encouragement.

(1) The remuneration policies, standards, and structure for the Company's 
Directors (including independent directors) are handled in accordance with 
the Articles of Incorporation and relevant internal regulations, and are 
designed to link with the Company's operating performance and sustainable 
development outcomes, as explained below: 
(a) Remuneration structure 
The Company's directors' remuneration includes transportation allowances 
(attendance fees), directors' remuneration, and necessary remuneration 
items: 
i. Transportation allowance: A fixed amount is paid per meeting based on the 
actual number of Board of Directors and related meetings attended by the 
director. 
ii. Directors' remuneration: According to Article 20 of the Company's Articles 
of Incorporation, if the Company makes a profit for the year, no more than 
2% of the profit shall be allocated as directors' remuneration. 
iii. Remuneration items: Relevant remuneration is granted based on the 
director's actual participation in duties and degree of contribution. 
(b) Remuneration determination mechanism 
The assessment of directors'  remuneration is comprehensively evaluated 
based on factors such as the director's scope of responsibilities, level of 
participation in the Company's operations, and time invested. It is then 
submitted to the Board of Directors for resolution after being r eviewed by 
the Remuneration Committee . (3) The assessment standard of Directors ’ 
remuneration and its correlation to operating performance 
(c) Criteria for Determining Director Remuneration and Its Correlation with 
Performance 
The Company's Director Remuneration is determined based on a 
comprehensive assessment of the following indicators, which serve as the 
basis for remuneration determination: 
i. Operating Performance (80% weight) 
 A comprehensive assessment based on overall performance across key 
indicators, including operating revenue, operating income, net profit before 
tax, and return on equity (ROE). 
ii. Sustainability Performance and External Benchmarks (10% weight) 
 Evaluated based on the achievement of the Company’s sustainability goals, 
covering dimensions such as corporate governance, ethical management, 
talent development, occupational safety and health, and environmental 
management, while also taking into account external indicators such as the 
results of the Corporate Governance Evaluation. 
17 
 
iii. Industry Standards (10% weight) 
 Reference to the remuneration levels of Directors in listed and OTC 
companies within the same industry to ensure market competitiveness and 
reasonableness. 
(d)  Correlation Between Remuneration and Operating Performance 
The Compa ny's Director Remuneration is determined based on the 
aforementioned comprehensive assessment mechanism. Due to a 
significant improvement in overall operating performance in 2025—
specifically, an 82.3% year-over-year (YoY) growth in operating revenue 
and a n 870% increase in net profit before tax —along with continued 
progress in sustainability performance, the Director remuneration for this 
year increased by approximately 193.93% compared to the previous year. 
The total remuneration represents approximately 3.1% of net profit after 
tax, demonstrating that the Company ’s Director compensation is 
reasonably correlated to overall operating results and corporate 
performance.

2. Industry Overview 
a. Industry Status and Development 
(1) Current Industry Status 
(a) Data center applications as the primary growth driver for the industry: 
The training and inference of generative AI rely heavily on massive, low-
latency data exchanges. Major global cloud service providers (CSPs) are 
continuously expanding their investments in data center infrastructure. 
This has driven explosive growth in demand for high-speed optical fiber 
interconnections between servers (server -to-server) and racks (rack -to-
rack), acting as the strongest engine propelling the expansion of the 
optical communication industry. 
(b) Continuous upgrading of high-speed transmission specifications: 
As the performance of AI computing chips continues to improve, optical 
transceiver module technology is rapidly transitioning from the 400G 
generation to 800G, while simultaneously accelerating the development 
and deployment of 1.6T and higher-speed specifications. 
(c) The rise of Silicon Photonics: 
As transmission rates escalate to 800G and beyond, traditional discrete 
optical components increasingly face technological bottlenecks in size, 
power consumption, and thermal dissipation. Silicon Photonics, which 
integrates opti cal components onto silicon chips, enhances overall 
integration and system efficiency, steadily emerging as a next-generation 
solution. However, since silicon inherently lacks light -emitting 
properties, the system must still rely on high -power, highly stab le 
Continuous Wave (CW) lasers as the critical light source. Consequently, 
III-V compound semiconductors (particularly Indium Phosphide, InP) 
 
78 
 
continue to play an indispensable strategic role in the optical 
communication supply chain. 
(2) Industry Development Trends 
The future development of the optical communication industry will exhibit a 
dual-track divergence between the Telecom and Datacom sectors. In the 
Telecom market, future growth will be primarily driven by replacement and 
optimization demands stemming from network capacity enhancements, 5G -
Advanced rollouts, and early-stage 6G deployments, leading to steady overall 
market growth. 
Conversely, Datacom applications are being propelled by the rapid expansion 
of generative AI and cloud computing demands. The continuous upgrading of 
data center infrastructure is fueling rapid growth in the need for high -speed 
optical interconnects. Consequently, optical transceiver module specifications 
are rapidly advancing from 400G to 800G, and are entering the 1.6T and 
higher-speed generations. This transition is simultaneously accelerating the 
adoption of next-generation technologies such as Silicon Photonics and Co -
Packaged Optics (CPO). As technology evolves, system requirements for 
high-power Continuous Wave (CW) lasers  and III -V compound 
semiconductor materials are rising in tandem, cementing epitaxy technology 
and high-end light sources as critical links in the supply chain. 
b. Relationship Between Upstream, Midstream, and Downstream Segments of the 
Industry 
The optical communication industry encompasses upstream materials and 
epitaxy, midstream component manufacturing and packaging, and downstream 
module assembly and system integration, with each segment being highly 
interconnected and interdependent. The Company is stra tegically positioned in 
the critical upstream technology segment, supplying high-quality epi-wafers that 
serve as the fundamental basis for downstream component performance and 
system applications. 
(1) Upstream (R&D and Manufacturing of Epi-wafers): 
Utilizing compound semiconductor materials such as Indium Phosphide (InP) 
and Gallium Arsenide (GaAs), the upstream segment employs precise 
epitaxial technologies like Metal -Organic Chemical Vapor Deposition 
(MOCVD) to grow epi-wafers with specific optoelectronic properties for the 
fabrication of optical communication components. Epitaxial quality, 
structural design, and process yield directly determine the performance, 
reliability, and transmission efficiency of laser components, acting as the core 
cornerstone of the entire optical communication supply chain. 
(2) Midstream (Component Processing and Packaging): 
Midstream manufacturers subject epi -wafers to semiconductor processes —
including etching, metallization, and die dicing —to fabricate active 
components such as laser  diodes (LDs) and photodetectors (PDs). These 
 
79 
 
components are then packaged into sub -modules, such as TO -CANs and 
optical sub -assemblies (OSAs), which serve as the core elements for 
downstream module products. 
(3) Downstream (Modules and Systems): 
Downstream equipment manufacturers integrate optical sub-assemblies with 
driver ICs and relevant electronic components to assemble optical 
transceiver modules. These modules are widely deployed across markets 
including data centers, telecommunication networks, and cons umer 
electronics.

F. Risk Management 
1. The effect on the Company’s profits (losses) of interest and exchange rate 
fluctuations and changes in the inflation rate, and response measures to be taken 
in the future: 
a. Changes in interest 
The Company has abundant private capital, and there is no need to loan from 
financial institutes. Changes in interest rate will not have significant impact on the 
Company’s operation. 
b. Changes in exchange rate 
A significant proportion of the Company ’s product is sold overseas, and the 
receivables of such cases would be calculated in U.S. dollars. The purchasing price 
of raw materials is also calculated in U.S. dollars. The loss from exchanging 
currencies in 2025 was NT$12,487 thousand, the percentage of the annual revenue 
was not significant. However, such impact of fluctuation in exchange rate may 
still influence the operation. So, the Company would continuously estimate and 
manage the revenue and expenses in U.S. dollars. It would also invest in derivative 
financial products like forward currency contracts to reduce the impact of 
fluctuation in exchange rate. 
c. Inflation 
There is no significant impact of inflation on the Company ’s operation so far. To 
stabilize the price, the C ompany maintain good relationship with suppliers and 
clients, adjust procurement and sales strategies to take proper measure in response 
of the changing situations, such as inflation, to avoid suffering from loss.

The Company maintains long -term partnerships with its major raw material 
suppliers and has secured two or more supply sources for certain key raw 
materials to ensure supply stability and meet production demands. In 2025 
(FY114), the Company ’s largest raw material supplier accounted for 
approximately 40.62% of total net purchases. This concentration was primarily 
driven by centralized procurement strategies aimed at reducing costs and 
enhancing supply chain management efficiency. 
To mitigate potential supply risks arising from procurement concentration, the 
Company has implemented the following primary countermeasures:  
(1) Continuously maintaining stable cooperative relationships with major 
suppliers and establishing long -term supply arrangements (e.g., signing 
long-term supply agreements) to secure the stability of supply sources.  
(2) Establishing robust inventory management and safety stock mechanisms to 
address short-term supply fluctuations. 
(3) Dynamically adjusting the procurement allocation among di fferent 
suppliers based on actual production and market demands to enhance the 
flexibility and stability of the supply chain. 
b. Risks Associated with Customer Concentration and Countermeasures 
In 2025, the Company's top three customers accounted for approximately 60.10% 
of its total operating revenue. The products sold to these key clients primarily
